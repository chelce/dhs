Hamilton: Opening Songs Task Cards

Character Analysis, Themes, and Historical Connections

EASY LEVEL (70%)
12-15 min
Hamilton's Big Dreams
In "Alexander Hamilton," we learn about Hamilton's goals and dreams. Work with your group to find what Hamilton wants most.

Directions:

  1. Listen to "Alexander Hamilton" again with your group
  2. Write down 3 things Hamilton wants to do or become
  3. Pick the most important dream and explain why it matters to him
  4. Share your answer with another group
Sentence Frame:
"Hamilton's biggest dream is to _______ because _______."

Helpful Hints:

  • Listen for words like "want," "will," and "gonna"
  • Think about what Hamilton says about his future
  • Pay attention to what makes Hamilton excited or passionate
EASY LEVEL (70%)
12-15 min
Aaron Burr's Advice
In "Aaron Burr, Sir," Burr gives Hamilton advice about how to act. Find out what Burr thinks Hamilton should do differently.

Directions:

  1. Listen to "Aaron Burr, Sir" with your group
  2. Find 2 pieces of advice Burr gives Hamilton
  3. Decide if this is good advice or bad advice
  4. Explain your choice using examples from the song
Sentence Frame:
"Burr tells Hamilton to _______, but I think this is _______ advice because _______."

Helpful Hints:

  • Look for times when Burr says "you should" or "don't"
  • Think about how Hamilton reacts to the advice
  • Consider what might happen if Hamilton follows or ignores the advice
EASY LEVEL (70%)
10-12 min
Hamilton's Energy
In "My Shot," Hamilton shows a lot of energy and excitement. Look for words and actions that show his personality.

Directions:

  1. Listen to "My Shot" and count how many times Hamilton says "I" or "I'm"
  2. Write down 3 action words (verbs) Hamilton uses
  3. Describe Hamilton's personality in 2-3 words
  4. Give examples from the song to support your description

Helpful Hints:

  • Action words tell us what someone does (write, fight, work)
  • Notice the speed and energy of Hamilton's singing
  • Think about words like: confident, excited, ambitious, impatient
EASY LEVEL (70%)
12-15 min
Friends and Dreams
In "The Story of Tonight," Hamilton and his friends talk about their hopes. Compare what the friends want with what Hamilton wants.

Directions:

  1. Listen to "The Story of Tonight" as a group
  2. Make two lists: What Hamilton wants vs. What his friends want
  3. Find one thing they all want together
  4. Discuss: Are they good friends? Why or why not?

Helpful Hints:

  • Listen for "we" words vs. "I" words
  • Pay attention to the chorus that they all sing together
  • Think about what makes people good friends
EASY LEVEL (70%)
10-12 min
The Schuyler Sisters' World
In "The Schuyler Sisters," we meet three sisters who have different personalities. Find out what makes each sister special.

Directions:

  1. Listen to "The Schuyler Sisters" with your group
  2. Write down one fact about each sister: Angelica, Eliza, and Peggy
  3. Choose which sister is most like you and explain why
  4. Discuss: What do all three sisters care about?

Helpful Hints:

  • Listen to what each sister says about herself
  • Notice how their voices and attitudes are different
  • Think about what they all agree on or want together
MEDIUM LEVEL (85%)
15 min
Hamilton vs. Burr: Leadership Styles
Compare how Hamilton and Burr approach leadership and making decisions. Analyze their different strategies and predict the consequences.

Directions:

  1. Review "Alexander Hamilton," "Aaron Burr, Sir," and "My Shot"
  2. Create a comparison chart: Hamilton's approach vs. Burr's approach
  3. Identify specific lyrics that show their leadership differences
  4. Predict which approach will be more successful and explain your reasoning

Helpful Hints:

  • Consider words like "wait," "speak," "action," and "silence"
  • Think about risk-taking vs. being cautious
  • Look for evidence of how others respond to each character

Directions:

Helpful Hints:

Directions:

  1. Deconstruct how "Alexander Hamilton" functions as both exposition and heroic myth-making
  2. Analyze the rhetorical strategies used to position Hamilton as simultaneously outsider and inevitable insider

  4. Synthesize how these narrative techniques might influence audience perception of historical figures and events

Helpful Hints:

  • Consider the role of the ensemble as both narrator and Greek chorus
  • Examine how contradictions (orphan/genius, outsider/founder) create compelling paradox
  • Analyze how musical theater conventions shape historical interpretation
